01 / Setup

Three steps, about four minutes.

No account, no sign-in, no wizard that phones home. Installing is the slow part — after that the app explains every permission it asks for, in plain words.

  1. 01

    Install the APK

    The APK installs straight from the page. The .zip is the same signed build with the README inside, for when you want the install steps offline. Android 12L or newer.

  2. 02

    Enable the accessibility service

    Android 13 and later hides accessibility behind an extra switch for sideloaded apps. In app info, open the menu and choose Allow restricted settings, then switch on the content filter service. The optional block service is separate.

  3. 03

    Set your budget

    Twenty minutes a day by default — one allowance shared by Instagram, YouTube and TikTok, reset at midnight. Per app you can also choose Filter, Block or Allow.

02 / Behaviour

A filter that knows the difference between browsing and watching.

Six behaviours, one idea: the app never decides what you should see. It decides when it is allowed to interrupt.

Direct engagement

The clock only starts when you do.

Scrolling past a reel is not a decision to watch one. Free Social classifies the surface, not your intent: feed cells and shelf cards are left completely alone. The moment Reels, Shorts or TikTok playback opens, a stopwatch runs — and stops the second you leave.

Shared budget

Twenty minutes. Three apps. One pot.

A single daily allowance across all short video, so switching apps is not a loophole. When it is gone, playback exits behind a calm flash — “Free Social blocked this” — never a wall, a countdown or a lecture.

Resets at midnightDuring locked schedule hours the budget counts as already spent.

Per app

Filter, Block, or Allow.

Filter hides short-video surfaces and leaves the app usable. Block closes the whole app during the hours you set. Allow leaves it untouched — useful when the problem is one habit, not all of them.

Nightly lock

A bedtime that is not a suggestion.

Set bedtime and wake time for each weekday, plus a wind-down before bed and a morning lock after waking. During locked hours the app is closed rather than merely quiet — and the budget cannot be spent.

Real exact alarms fire on each boundary, so the lock flips while the phone is idle in your pocket.

On-device maintenance

Broken rule? Rebuild it on the phone.

When a screen changes, guided node capture walks you to the right view and dumps the live hierarchy. Patch the rule override in-app, save it, and the running service picks it up immediately — no restart, no cable, no second device.

03 / Honest rules

Built on honest rules.

A blocker that finds Reels by looking for the word “Reels” works in exactly one language and breaks on the first rename. This one matches structure — and shows you the working.

Free Social finds short video by structure, not by wording. Each rule is one small claim about the screen: this container, with this id, in this app. So a phone set to Spanish filters like one set to English — and when a rule stops being true, the app says so instead of guessing.

How does it recognise a reel?

By an anchor — a landmark in the app's view tree that stays put. Three kinds count: a resource id like …:id/clips_viewer_view_pager, a class name like androidx.viewpager2…, or the bounds of a container that fills the screen. Those are the same in every language build, because the app has to keep them for its own code to run.

Visible text is a different thing. “Reels”, “Shorts”, “Para ti”: all of it is translated, renamed and sometimes absent. Text is allowed as a hint — it can narrow a match — but it is never the proof on its own. The shipped catalog is graded that way:

view-id="…:id/clips_viewer_view_pager"proof
classNameRegex="androidx.viewpager2…"proof
bounds · full-screen containerproof
contentDescription="Reels"optional hint
visible textnever the only proof

Six of the eight shipped rules are enabled; the other two are retired evidence, kept disabled on purpose. The build fails if an enabled rule has no locale-independent anchor, or if that anchor is not cited against a recorded XML dump.

One concrete example. YouTube's Shorts shelf has no view-id at all — it is a plain container inside the home feed. That rule matches the container's shape and treats the word “Shorts” as a hint, never as proof. Tests pin it against captured trees from English, German and Portuguese builds, and the catalog records it as the exception it is.

What happens when Instagram updates?

The rules fail open — on purpose. In plain words: if a rule stops matching, the filter stops filtering and lets that screen through. It does not block the wrong thing, and it does not pretend everything is fine. A reel that leaks is annoying; an app that closes your DMs is worse.

So Free Social keeps a stamp: the app version each rule set was verified against. It compares that with the version installed, and when the app is newer, you get a warning with exactly one action on it — capture the new screen and re-verify. No silent failure, no invented certainty.

How do I fix a rule myself?

A rule is never written from a PC, a cable or a scrape of the store listing. It is written from the screen itself, on the phone that has the app installed — and that is the same path you take when an update breaks one.

  1. 01Open the screen you want recognised in the target app.
  2. 02Arm guided capture. Progress and “Stop now” live in a notification; the dump records view-ids, text, bounds and classes.
  3. 03Save it as a rule override, editable in-app. The running service re-reads it on the spot.

The result is maintenance, not magic: no restart, no rebuild, no second device. The override beats the built-in rule until you switch back to the built-in set, which is also what a proper fix in the next release looks like.

04 / Privacy

No accounts. No analytics. No socket.

This is a permission list, not a policy page. You can check every line of it inside the APK you downloaded.

There is no backend to trust, because there is no backend. Nothing about you leaves the phone.

Settings live in SharedPreferences on the device. Rule sets are assets inside the APK. Every decision is made in-process, from the view tree that is on screen at that moment.

There is no sign-up, no crash reporter, no analytics SDK and no ad library. There is nothing to delete, because nothing was collected.

<!-- AndroidManifest.xml -->
<uses-permission android:name="…SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW" />
<uses-permission android:name="…USE_EXACT_ALARM" />
<uses-permission android:name="…POST_NOTIFICATIONS" />

<!-- no android.permission.INTERNET anywhere -->

No write access, no network stack, no remote anything: the app cannot open a socket, so there is nothing to leak.

INTERNET absent
Not requested. No uploads, no sync, no remote config, no usage ping.
BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE content filter
Walks the view hierarchy while Instagram, YouTube or TikTok is on screen. Other apps' trees are only ever read by the ghost-tree self-check, which asks whether a target is hiding content — nothing is stored.
BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE block service
Window changes only; content retrieval is switched off in its config. Reads a package name and nothing else.
SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW
Draws the 2.2-second escape flash over the app. It cannot stack, cannot linger and has no dismiss button.
REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS
Optional, asked once. Keeps the service from being frozen mid-evening by an aggressive power profile.
USE_EXACT_ALARM
Schedule boundaries fire on time — bedtime, wake, wind-down, morning lock.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS
Progress and “Stop now” during node capture. Denial is handled in-app; capture still works.
<queries> · 4 packages
Instagram, YouTube, TikTok (global and trill). Used to launch the app while capturing a screen.

No contacts, no location, no storage, no camera, no microphone, no AD_ID.

05 / Limits

What this doesn't do.

Every tool has edges. These are the ones you will actually meet — stated before you install, not after.

  • 01

    Rules drift after app updates

    An app update renames a view and the rule stops matching: the reel shows, and the app tells you why instead of pretending. Fixing it takes a node capture, not a new release — but it is maintenance, and calling it magic would be a lie.

  • 02

    Battery savers kill accessibility services

    Some Android builds freeze background services within minutes of the screen going off. Free Social asks for a battery exemption once, then shows a persistent banner when enforcement is degraded. It cannot force the issue, and it will not claim to.

  • 03

    Sideload only

    No Play Store listing. You install an APK and grant a permission that reads as alarming — because it is powerful. You give it to three apps, and you can revoke it in Settings whenever you like.

  • 04

    It filters short video, not the feed

    Your feed, DMs, comments and search are untouched, on purpose. This is not a screen-time tracker, a DNS blocker or a parental control. It is one specific intervention, done carefully.
